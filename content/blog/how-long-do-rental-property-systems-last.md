Every rental has a handful of systems that will need replacing someday: the roof, the furnace and AC, the water heaters, the appliances, the flooring. The hard part isn't knowing that they'll wear out. It's knowing roughly *when*, so the cost doesn't land as a surprise.

Here are typical lifespans for the systems that matter most, and how to use them.

## Typical lifespans

These are common ranges, in years. Your building may land outside them, and the next section explains why.

### Roofing

- **Asphalt shingle roof:** about 20 to 25
- **Flat or membrane roof:** about 15 to 25
- **Gutters and downspouts:** about 20 to 30

### Heating and cooling

- **Gas furnace:** about 15 to 20
- **Central AC or condenser:** about 12 to 15
- **Heat pump:** about 12 to 15
- **Boiler:** about 20 to 30

### Plumbing

- **Tank water heater:** about 8 to 12
- **Tankless water heater:** about 15 to 20

### Appliances

- **Refrigerator:** about 10 to 15
- **Range or oven:** about 13 to 15
- **Dishwasher:** about 9 to 12
- **Washer and dryer:** about 10 to 13

### Floors and finishes

- **Carpet:** about 5 to 10
- **Luxury vinyl plank:** about 15 to 25
- **Interior paint:** about 3 to 7, often at turnover

The [component lifespan chart](/lifespan) has these and more, with a page for each system.

## Why your building may not match

A lifespan range is an average across a lot of buildings. Yours is one building. A few things push it one way or the other:

- **Climate.** Hot summers are hard on AC units and roofs. Long winters work furnaces harder.
- **Water quality.** Hard water shortens the life of water heaters and dishwashers.
- **Install quality.** A well-installed system with proper venting and drainage tends to outlast a rushed one.
- **Maintenance.** Changing filters, flushing water heaters and clearing gutters really does add years.
- **Tenant turnover.** Frequent move-outs are hard on carpet, paint and appliances.

So treat the range as a starting point, not a promise. If your water heater is 10 years old and the area has hard water, plan as if it's near the end, even though 10 is inside the range.

## How to find the age of what you have

You can't plan without knowing how old things are. A few places to look:

- **Labels and serial numbers.** Most water heaters, furnaces and AC units have a manufacture date printed on the label or encoded in the serial number.
- **Closing documents and inspection reports.** These often list the age or condition of major systems.
- **Receipts and permits.** If a system was replaced, the invoice or permit has the date.
- **Your best estimate.** If none of that exists, an honest guess is better than a blank. You can update it later.

## Turning lifespans into a plan

Once you have an age and a typical lifespan for each system, you can see roughly when each one will come due. Put them on a timeline and two things usually stand out:

1. **Something is closer than you thought.** Often a water heater or an AC unit that's quietly near the end.
2. **Several things line up in the same few years.** Systems installed together tend to wear out together, which can make for one very expensive stretch.

Seeing that ahead of time is the whole point. It gives you years, not days, to set money aside.

## Keep it current

Lifespans don't change much, but your building does. When you replace something, update its install date. When you get a new quote, update the cost. A once-a-year check keeps the plan honest without much work.
